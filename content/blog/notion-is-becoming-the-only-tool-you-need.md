I've been a Notion user for only a few months now, but something shifted recently. What used to be my note-taking app has quietly become the single tool I reach for throughout the entire day. Not because I forced it, but because Notion kept getting better at doing things I used to need five other apps for. At some point I looked up and realized: I don't really need anything else.
## Everything lives in one place
My expense tracking? Notion database. Job hunt pipeline? Notion board. Email triage? Notion Mail. Blog writing and publishing? You're reading it. Ideation, research, search across all my tools, even planning my weekends. It all happens in Notion.
This isn't about Notion being the *best* at any single thing. My spreadsheet app had more formulas. My email client had better keyboard shortcuts. But the compounding value of having everything in one connected workspace is hard to overstate. When my job applications database can reference my notes on a company, which can pull from emails I've exchanged with a recruiter, which feeds into a weekly review I generate automatically, that's when the whole becomes greater than the sum of its parts.
## Templates bridged the gap, AI closed it
I'll be honest: I wasn't always a Notion person. I used it here and there over the years, but every time I'd find myself quitting. The tool was powerful, but the setup friction was real. You had to understand databases, properties, views, relations, and wire everything together yourself.
Templates were the bridge. You could go to the marketplace, find a beautifully designed expense tracker or project board, and start from there. And you could tell that real thought and quality went into those templates. People built entire businesses around them, Notion consultants and experts charged good money to set up workspaces, and it made sense because it was genuinely hard to do yourself.
But templates had a fundamental problem: they weren't *yours*. You'd start with someone else's massive system and then spend hours downsizing it, removing properties you didn't need, restructuring views that didn't match your workflow. The template gave you a starting point, but the hand-holding and customization work to make it actually fit your needs was still on you.
That's where I think most people get Notion wrong. They grab a template, get overwhelmed by someone else's system, and quit. I did the same thing, multiple times. What I actually wanted was something minimal, clean, and built around *my* workflow, not a pre-built system I had to reverse-engineer.
Notion AI flipped that entirely. Instead of starting from a bloated template and trimming down, you start from zero and build up. Tell the AI you want an expense tracker and it builds one from scratch, tailored to what you actually need. Want to change something? Just tell it. No more dragging blocks around or googling "how to make a Notion rollup." The consultants, the template marketplace, the YouTube setup guides, AI makes all of that unnecessary. You just ask, and it sets everything up.
And here's the thing that makes it a complete no-brainer: Notion's Business plan is \$20 a month. That's the same price as a ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro subscription. Except with Notion, you get access to all of those models, Gemini, Claude, GPT, with no usage limits on AI. I genuinely don't open ChatGPT anymore. Every model I need is right here, inside the tool where all my work already lives. Why would I pay \$20 for a standalone chatbot when I can pay the same amount and get the chatbot *plus* an entire workspace that connects everything together?
## Why Notion AI beats standalone chatbots for me
I used to keep a ChatGPT tab open all day. For brainstorming, summarizing, drafting, you name it. But there was always this friction: I had to copy context out of Notion, paste it into ChatGPT, get a response, then paste it back. Every single time.
Notion AI eliminated that loop entirely. It already has my notes, my databases, my documents. When I ask it something, it doesn't need me to explain who I am or what I'm working on. It *knows*, because all of that context already lives in the workspace.
The more I use Notion, the better this gets. Every new page, every database entry, every meeting note adds to the context that Notion AI can draw from. ChatGPT and Gemini can only infer things from my chat history. Notion AI has the actual artifacts of my work and life.
And it's not like I'm giving up model quality for convenience. Notion now gives you access to top-tier models, including Claude Opus 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, with unlimited AI usage on paid plans. There's no separate \$20/month subscription to worry about. It's just there, built into the tool I'm already using.
## The first truly agentic text editor
This is the part that changed everything for me. Notion isn't just an AI *assistant* anymore. It's becoming an AI *collaborator* that takes action.
With Custom Agents, launched in February 2026, Notion introduced something genuinely new: AI that doesn't just respond to prompts but proactively does work on your behalf. You can set up agents that trigger on a schedule, when a database entry is created, when a Slack message comes in, or when you mention them on a page. They read your workspace, make edits, update databases, and draft content.
This blog post is a perfect example. I created this page, jotted down some rough notes about what I wanted to say, and a custom agent picked it up, researched the topic, and drafted the full post. I didn't copy-paste anything. I didn't switch tabs. I just wrote my thoughts and let the agent do the heavy lifting.
That's what I mean by "agentic text editor." You write, and the AI edits, expands, and structures for you. No other writing tool works like this right now.
## Cursor, but for docs
If you're a developer, you probably know Cursor, the AI-powered code editor that understands your codebase and can make changes across files. Notion is becoming the equivalent of that, but for documents and knowledge work.
Just like Cursor indexes your code and uses it as context for suggestions, Notion indexes your workspace and uses it as context for everything. Ask it to draft a project brief and it pulls from your existing docs. Ask it to summarize your week and it reads your meeting notes, tasks, and updates. The more structured your workspace, the better the output.
And with MCP (Model Context Protocol) integrations, the boundaries keep expanding. I use Claude Opus 4.6 to plan my code implementations. It can agentically create task lists in Notion and connect to Claude Code and OpenCode for development context over MCP. The planning happens in Notion, the execution happens in the terminal, and they talk to each other.
## It helps me think better
This might sound abstract, but it's the reason I keep coming back. Notion has become my thinking environment.
When I brain-dump ideas into a page, AI can help me organize them into something coherent. When I'm stuck on a decision, I can talk it through with the agent and it will reference my own past notes and reasoning. When I'm researching a topic, it searches across my workspace *and* the web, then synthesizes everything in one place.
It's not replacing my thinking. It's augmenting it. And because it knows my context, the augmentation actually feels relevant rather than generic.
## The compounding effect
The real unlock with Notion isn't any single feature. It's the compounding effect of putting everything in one place and having AI that gets smarter the more you use it.
Every note you take makes search better. Every database you build gives agents more to work with. Every document you write becomes context for future AI responses. It's a flywheel that rewards consistent use.
A standalone chatbot starts every conversation from zero (or close to it). Notion AI starts every conversation with years of your accumulated knowledge, projects, and ideas. That gap only widens over time.
## Is it perfect?
No. There are still moments where I wish I had a dedicated tool for something specific. The email experience is still maturing. Some advanced spreadsheet workflows are easier in a purpose-built app. And if you need deep, multi-turn reasoning on a novel problem with no workspace context, a standalone model might still edge ahead.
But for the 90% of my digital life that involves writing, organizing, planning, tracking, and thinking, Notion handles it all. And it keeps getting better at a pace that's hard to ignore.
