I woke up today with nothing to do.
No deadlines. No assignments. No standups. Just my ceiling fan and the sound of traffic outside. For the first time in my life, I had zero obligations.
Most people dream about having nothing on their plate. I finally got it. And honestly? It scared me.
## The silence after the finish line
Last week, I wrapped up my internship at Better Age Solutions as Lead Mobile Developer, through the Singapore Institute of Technology and the University of Glasgow. I'm grateful for every bit of trust and opportunity I got along the way.
But when it was over, what I felt wasn't relief. It was this strange, unsettling quiet.
Psychologists have a name for this. It's called the *arrival fallacy*, a term coined by Tal Ben-Shahar, a positive psychology researcher and former Harvard lecturer. It's the belief that once you achieve a specific goal, you'll finally feel happy, content, settled. But research consistently shows that the emotional lift from reaching a goal is brief. The mind moves on to the next thing almost immediately.
For high achievers especially, the emptiness after a big milestone can feel like a private failure. You've done the thing everyone told you to do. You should be celebrating. Instead, there's just... silence.
Neuroscience backs this up. As we pursue goals, our brains release dopamine, the so-called "achievement hormone." It keeps us focused, creative, alert. But once the goal is reached, dopamine levels drop. The very thing that drove us forward disappears, and we're left wondering why the finish line feels so anticlimactic.
## Ten minutes of silence
For me, the silence lasted about ten minutes.
Because I'm not here to rest. I'm here to build.
I've been working nonstop since I was 15. Through school, through holidays, through weekends. Not because someone told me to, but because building things is the only way I know how to process the world.
Over the last decade, I've built recognizable brands that served many:
- [**titan.tf**](http://titan.tf), a gaming community and platform I started from my bedroom at 15. It hit 470,000 users. I didn't even have a bank account yet.
- [**supply.tf**](http://supply.tf), a streetwear brand born from one belief: stand out, don't blend in. Chase dreams against all odds.
- [**decosmic.com**](http://decosmic.com), where I was building what we now call RAG before the term existed, anchoring LLMs on trusted data.
- [**base07.com**](http://base07.com), a product studio I co-founded focused on privacy-first software and agentic apps.
- [**updatenight.com**](http://updatenight.com), a newsletter and podcast for builders who aren't covered by mainstream tech media.
Looking at that list, I realize the emptiness wasn't really emptiness at all. It was a gap between chapters. And gaps are where the interesting stuff happens.
## Why builders fear the blank calendar
There's a particular kind of anxiety that comes with an empty calendar. It's not laziness. It's the opposite. When your identity is tied to what you create and ship, a day with nothing scheduled feels like a day without purpose.
Psychologists describe this as the hedonic treadmill, our tendency to return to a baseline level of happiness regardless of what happens to us. Good or bad, we adapt. Which means the satisfaction of finishing something big fades fast, and we're left reaching for the next thing.
But here's what I've learned: the reaching isn't the problem. The problem is when you reach without pausing to notice where you are.
An empty calendar doesn't mean you've lost direction. It means nothing's in the way.
## What's next
So here's what I'm doing with all this space:
1. Going full-time as CEO of A Major. I'm really excited to unveil what's coming at [amajor.ai](http://amajor.ai).
2. Going full-time on YouTube. No fixed schedule yet, but expect content almost every day. Short form coming soon.
3. Giving talks at IRL events in Singapore.
4. More brand announcements incoming.
I built [supply.tf](http://supply.tf) on one belief: stand out, don't blend in. SUPPLY the future, don't wait for it.
That belief doesn't change just because the calendar is empty. If anything, it gets louder.
The arrival fallacy tells us we'll be happy when we get *there*. But "there" keeps moving. The real trick isn't arriving. It's learning to feel full in the spaces between.
Today felt empty. But it's the fullest kind of empty I've ever known.
