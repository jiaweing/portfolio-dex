Here's an interesting story about my startup, Ryu.
For the longest time I went around pitching Ryu to anyone who would listen. The truth is I didn't have a product, or anything built at all. This goes as far back as two years ago.
I was everywhere, selling an idea that was nothing more than an idea. I pitched it to as many people as I could and used every conversation to gather what people actually needed most. Most of them were genuinely interested in trying it out, but I had nothing to give them.
It sounds contradictory. Why would you sell something you don't have yet? It feels like a complete scam, and honestly it kind of is. But it's also the fastest way to find out whether your idea is even worth pursuing. If nobody wants it, why bother building it in the first place?
I didn't rush in and start building. Building things became easy in the era of AI. Anyone can build now, so the moat is no longer in building. Everyone copies everyone, someone ships a new feature and by tomorrow ten other apps have the same one. The cost of shipping has dropped to zero, and that's exactly why all the apps are starting to look the same. Part of it is a UX thing too, familiarity just skips the learning curve.
So I validated first. I applied to the BLOCK71 Spring 2026 residency without a product, just a solid, sound idea backed by proven validation that this was worth building. I had a simple initial prototype of Ryu built last year, but I never showed a demo of it to anyone.
I didn't waste a single day building things people didn't want. And now I'm building the restaurant to feed the line of hungry people already waiting outside the door.
